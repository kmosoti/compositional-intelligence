# HYP-0003: Functional implicit and explicit coordination

**Status:** proposed / untested. **Primary protocol:** [EXP-0003](../experiments/EXP-0003/protocol.md).

## Claim and competing explanation

Selective information sharing and uncertainty-triggered deliberation improve accepted task success under bounded resources relative to competitive fixed policies and a development-frequency random control. A lightweight local process may act directly when evidence suffices and request broader computation when its uncertainty or predicted failure cost crosses a learned or engineered criterion.

**Null:** Extra computation or information access explains the gain; random or simple fixed rules perform equally well; the added coordination introduces stale decisions, overhead, or correlated errors.

## Operational meaning

Here **implicit** means a local task process whose intermediate state is not automatically shared. **Explicit** means selected evidence made available to other task processes. These are experimental information-access labels. They do not designate an unconscious mind, a conscious supervisor, model size, biological homology, or a permanent architectural hierarchy.

The [C0/C1/C2 framework](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aan8871) motivates separating processing, broad availability, and self-monitoring. [Theory-derived consciousness indicators](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2025.10.011) remain conditional on contested theories and applicability assumptions. Functional engineering tests here make no inference about subjective experience.

## Measurements and disconfirmers

Measure paired accepted success, stale actions, communication volume, verification cost, and confidence's usefulness for allocating computation. Compare adaptive escalation with always, never, simple threshold, and development-frequency random policies. The primary comparison concerns policy utility under common caps; development-derived counts need not match realized holdout verification counts. A stronger claim about the causal benefit of allocation requires a prospectively specified realized-count replay or controlled allocation design.

Score confidence against an independent offline label for each original pre-escalation proposal in every episode, including proposals the actor never verifies. Repaired answers must not supply the original proposal's label. Keep these labels outside the actor's decision path and disclose evaluation costs separately. Shuffle confidence to test whether its information matters. Hold task observations, total resource caps, and acceptance rules fixed.

Reject the tested mechanism if gain disappears under compute matching, a simple controller dominates, confidence does not predict useful verification, or selective sharing primarily suppresses decisive evidence. A positive result supports the particular coordination mechanism on the declared task distribution.

**No results exist.** The implementation is a replaceable experimental instrument. Freeze individual run protocols, not future architecture.

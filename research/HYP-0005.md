# HYP-0005: Recoverable context and deterministic work

**Status:** proposed, untested. [Protocol](../experiments/EXP-0005/protocol.md).

## Hypothesis

For bounded software tasks using the same native LLM harness, recoverable evidence projections and preauthorized deterministic tool sequences can reduce measured lifecycle resources while preserving independently checked success within a prospectively declared quality margin and without increasing critical policy failures.

## Mechanisms and alternatives

Receipts may avoid repeatedly presenting settled detail; exact retrieval may recover decisive information; deterministic sequences may remove repetitive model coordination. Conversely, projection may obscure relevant evidence, retrieval may increase requests/output, and batching may interfere with cache reuse or sensible stopping.

A simpler native harness with ordinary tools and retrieval may already occupy the better quality-cost point. Savings may instead arise from a long-lived-session baseline, differing tools, or incomplete accounting.

## Predictions

Test receipts R, deterministic flow D, and RD separately against B0. Distinguish fresh-task from sequential-session effects. Exact byte recovery is an instrument requirement; decision recovery and held-out quality are behavioral outcomes.

## Rejection or narrowing

Reject an adoption claim if quality fails the frozen criterion, policy failures increase unacceptably, or savings disappear after failures/retrieval/checking/maintenance are counted. Restrict any finding to the tested regime and accessible usage categories. Failure to reject a quality difference is not proof of parity.

## Evidence state

HELIX provides external exploratory motivation, not our baseline, an independently reproduced outcome, or proof of this hypothesis. See the [source review](../docs/research/helix-context-review.md). No model experiment has been run for HYP-0005.
